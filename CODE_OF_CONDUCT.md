# Code of Conduct

Volura is made for makers: people who design things and print them. Everyone is welcome here,
whatever their experience with 3D modelling, programming or printing. This code of conduct applies
to everything around the project: issues, pull requests, Discussions, commits and code review.

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Volura to be a friendly, safe and harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with beginners. Every expert once asked how to extrude a face.
- Assume good intent. Text loses tone; ask before you take offence.
- Give feedback on the work, not on the person, and say what would make it better.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no to an idea, and why. It is about what fits Volura, not about
  you.
- Credit the work of others, and only share models, code and images that you may share.
- Keep discussions on topic, and in English, so everyone can follow and help.

## What we do not accept

- Insults, personal or political attacks, trolling, and derogatory or discriminatory remarks.
- Sexualised language or images, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Harassment, in public or in private, including continuing to contact someone who asked you to
  stop.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as an address or e-mail, without their
  explicit permission.
- Spam, advertising unrelated to Volura, and posts meant to trick people or automated tools into
  doing something harmful, such as hidden instructions or malicious links.
- Anything else that would reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a friendly, professional space.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that breaks this code of conduct, report it to the maintainer,
[@Lexvdpoel](https://github.com/Lexvdpoel). You can use GitHub's **Report content** option in the
menu of any issue, comment or discussion, or contact the maintainer privately through the contact
details on that GitHub profile. Every report is read and handled confidentially.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what to do about behaviour that breaks this code of conduct. Depending on
what happened, that can be:

1. **A note**: a private message that explains what was wrong, and possibly a request to apologise.
2. **A warning**: with consequences for continued behaviour, such as no contact with the people
   involved for a while.
3. **A temporary ban**: no taking part in the project for a set period.
4. **A permanent ban**: for repeated or serious violations, such as harassment.

Posts that break this code of conduct may be edited, hidden or removed, and threads may be locked.
